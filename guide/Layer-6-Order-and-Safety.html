Layer 6 · Order and Safety

A rule an assistant can talk itself out of is not a rule

An assistant told to be careful will sometimes not be. The restriction has to sit somewhere it cannot be talked out of.

What the last layer left you with

You have several assistants, each capable of forming a view and drafting something. Any one of them could put a message in front of a real person. Nothing decides what order they run in. Nothing counts them against a shared limit. And any one of them can approve its own work.

What this layer builds

The rules that make all of that safe, built into how your system runs rather than written into what you asked for. The most important one: nothing reaches another human being without you.

Layer 6 · How it works

How it works

5 rules follow. Each one is enforced by the software rather than requested in the instructions.

1. The one that wrote it never approves it

For the same reason you would not mark your own homework. A separate assistant reads the draft cold, without knowing who wrote it, and can reject it.

2. Limits are counted across everything at once

If you allow yourself a number of messages a day, that number applies to your whole system, not to each part of it separately. 3 well-behaved activities each staying under the limit can add up to a breach between them, and the platform you are messaging on counts the total, not your good intentions.

3. One job at a time

Only one part of your system may use a logged-in connection at a time. 2 jobs using the same one at once is how you get signed out, or locked out.

4. If you message someone yourself, the system backs off

The moment you take a conversation over personally, everything automatic for that person stops until you say otherwise. Nothing should be talking over you, and nothing should send a polite follow-up in front of you for someone you had a real conversation with the day before.

This one is worth setting up properly, because the failure it prevents is embarrassing in front of exactly the people you most want to impress.

5. Nothing reaches a person without you

Your system prepares, drafts, checks and queues. It does not send. The final action is yours, every time, and there is no setting to change that. Anything automatic in here writes to your own records; anything pointed at another human being waits for you.

Layer 6 · Before you start

Before you start on a Mac

Everything in this guide is typed into Terminal, the Mac's window for typing commands instead of clicking. Press Command and Space together, type Terminal, and press Return.

This layer needs Python and git, which you set up for Layer 1 (its page "Before you start on a Mac"), and the layers before this one. It installs no add-ons, so it works with either Python a Mac may have.

How to type the lines

Where this guide shows several lines in a black box, type them one at a time: type the first, press Return, wait for it to finish and give you a fresh line, then type the next. The installer asks you questions. Each offers an answer in [square brackets]; press Return to take it, or type your own and press Return.

Where your CRM is

Layer 1 made your CRM at ~/CRM: a folder called CRM in your home folder (the folder with your name and the house picture in Finder's sidebar). If you chose another folder in Layer 1, put your own folder wherever a line in this guide says ~/CRM. The installer finds your CRM by itself either way.

Apple Silicon or Intel (the 2 kinds of chip a Mac can have; the Apple menu, then About This Mac, shows yours): the steps are the same on both, and both were tested.

Tried only on test Macs. Every step in this guide and in the instructions file of the layer's download was tried on test Macs (Macs GitHub rents out by the minute to run a script, not a person's own Mac, and not yours), never on a real Mac, except 1: the box asking to install the developer tools, which cannot appear on a test Mac. The last pages list every line and what happened to it.

Layer 6 · Install

Build it

3 lines, typed into Terminal. The last one asks you a few questions so what you get is yours, then does the work.
git clone https://github.com/OUTLIERS-ai/outliers-crm-06-safety-mac
cd outliers-crm-06-safety-mac
python3 install.py

This layer needs Layers 1 to 5. It changes how the assistants from Layer 5 run.

What it asks you, and why

QuestionWhat it changes
How many messages a day is sensible in your world?Used as the shared limit across everything your system does. Set it low; you can raise it.
Is there anyone who must never be contacted automatically?Clients mid-project, friends, anyone sensitive. They go on your do-not-contact list for good, and nothing will draft or queue anything aimed at them.

What you end up with

  • An order your jobs run in, and what happens when a step fails.
  • A separate check on anything written, by something that did not write it.
  • Shared limits, so parts of your system cannot breach a cap between them.
  • A do-not-contact list, and the rule that nothing sends without you.

Layer 6 · Finishing

Now use it

2 minutes, and it is the only way to trust it.

Put someone on your do-not-contact list. Then tell your system to prepare a message for them.

The tools this layer installed live in a folder called _engine inside your CRM. In Terminal, go there first, then type the next 2 lines (if your CRM is not at ~/CRM, put your own folder in the cd line):

cd ~/CRM/_engine
python3 holds.py hold "Rowan Ashdown" "taking this one myself"
python3 sendgate.py "Rowan Ashdown"

It refuses, and tells you why.

Try it with the profile link instead of the name. It still refuses, because the do-not-contact entry is on the person rather than on one spelling of them, which is the Layer 2 identity work paying off in a place you would not have predicted.

Once you have seen it refuse, you know the list works, rather than assuming it does.

If you already had something like this

Your test: put someone on a do-not-contact list in your current setup, then try to reach them through a different route than the one you used to list them. If it lets you, your list is on a piece of text rather than on a person.

What this layer leaves unsolved

Everything is safe and orderly now, and it works through its queue in the order the queue was built. Something that arrived this morning waits behind a list made last month. Nothing here knows the difference between urgent and merely present.
